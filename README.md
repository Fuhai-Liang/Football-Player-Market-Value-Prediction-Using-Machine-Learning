# Football-Player-Market-Value-Prediction-Using-Machine-Learning

This project aims to study how on-field performance and off-field popularity jointly determine the market value of a football player, and to provide a transparent and repeatable prediction and analysis process. A multi-dimensional dataset was collected, covering general descriptive information (name, nationality, club), performance indicators (ratings, goal count, assist count), popularity indicators (media and fan signals), financial attributes (value, salary, release clause), and physical characteristics (age, height, weight). After cleaning, filling in missing values, and performing category encoding, the market value was divided into balanced intervals to reduce the phenomenon of class imbalance and to be consistent with the actual transfer market range. 

In terms of methodology, three complementary models were employed: a decision tree to enhance interpretability, a random forest to enhance robustness through ensemble methods, and a "one-to-one" logistic regression model to provide a probability benchmark. Hyperparameters were adjusted through cross-validation; we report accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and ROC-AUC to capture discrimination ability and the balance between imbalanced classes. In addition to these metrics, we also study feature contribution and position differences, while recognizing factors such as popularity effect and league reputation that may lead to bias. The goal is not only to rank the models, but also to understand when each method succeeds or fails under real data constraints. 

The target users include analysts, club decision-makers, investors, and students. Analysts can apply the code to other football datasets or related sports markets. Clubs can combine these insights with scouting work to support transfer valuations and salary negotiations. Investors can quantify risks by comparing performance signals with attention-driven premiums. Educators can use this project as a brief case study in supervised learning, model selection, and fairness considerations. Future work will include more comprehensive labels, time dynamics, and causal sensitivity analysis.
